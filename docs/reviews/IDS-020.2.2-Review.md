## Scope Verdict

**PASS**

The IDS is limited to:

- explicit governed Context relationships;
- relationship identity, meaning, direction, responsibility, and lifecycle;
- Interface Commitment identity and information-delivery lifecycle;
- provider and consumer accountability;
- Project and same-Project cross-Workspace scope;
- authority and responsibility boundaries;
- authorization and source confidentiality;
- audit atomicity;
- optimistic concurrency;
- Workspace Core and Core Context compatibility;
- additive migration and PostgreSQL validation obligations where persisted
  state is required.

The initial relationship boundary is finite and engineering-specific:

- requires;
- provided by;
- consumed by;
- potentially affects.

Existing applies-to and evidenced-by meaning remains owned by PATCH-020.2.1
subject and source references rather than being duplicated.

## Non-Scope Verdict

**PASS**

The IDS explicitly excludes:

- AI behavior, AI Insights, and AI-generated relationships;
- automatic dependency or impact inference;
- Derived Context;
- Missing Information;
- Conflict Engine behavior or resolution;
- Search;
- Historical Context and snapshots;
- Human Review implementation;
- Engineering Decision Log;
- Engineering Execution Plan;
- Engineering Health and Workspace Readiness;
- Knowledge Graph;
- workflow, tasks, notifications, schedules, reminders, and escalation;
- frontend and transport-interface design;
- new roles or competence certification;
- later PATCH-020.2 capability.

ENSE and Engineering Memory are not named individually in the non-scope list,
but neither is authorized anywhere in Scope, Required Domain Objects, Required
Behaviors, or Definition of Done. The general exclusion of later
PATCH-020.2 capabilities keeps both outside this IDS. Naming both explicitly
would improve defensive clarity but is not an architectural blocker.

## ADR-015 Alignment

**PASS**

The IDS preserves ADR-015 principles:

- relationships are explicit, governed statements;
- directional meaning is retained where provider, consumer, requirement, or
  impact semantics require direction;
- relationships do not transfer ownership, authority, maturity, approval, or
  source access;
- Interface Commitments remain information-delivery dependencies rather than
  workflow;
- provider delivery is separate from consumer engineering acceptance;
- cross-discipline accountability retains source, revision, confidentiality,
  stewardship, and change impact;
- human responsibility and competence boundaries remain intact.

No unlimited relationship vocabulary, generic graph, automatic inference, or
AI-managed relationship behavior is authorized.

## EDS-020.2 Alignment

**PASS WITH MINOR CORRECTIONS REQUIRED**

The IDS correctly narrows EDS-020.2 to PATCH-020.2.2 and retains the accepted
Interface Commitment states:

- Identified;
- Acknowledged by Provider;
- Information Provided;
- Consumer Review Required;
- Fulfilled for Stated Use;
- Rejected;
- Disputed;
- Superseded.

It preserves the EDS rules that acknowledgement is not completeness, provision
is not acceptance, fulfilment is bounded to stated use and revision, material
revision change requires reassessment, and provider disagreement is not
resolved by this patch.

The IDS must clarify governed commitment withdrawal because its validation
contract calls for commitment lifecycle completeness but defines withdrawal
only for the surrounding relationship lifecycle. The correction must remain
consistent with the accepted EDS state model and must not invent workflow or
historical reconstruction.

## Core Context Compatibility

**PASS**

The IDS:

- depends on accepted PATCH-020.2.1;
- reuses Core Context identity, scope, responsibility, authority, source,
  lifecycle, audit, and version foundations;
- does not duplicate applies-to or evidenced-by representations;
- keeps linked Context and Workspace versions independent;
- preserves existing behavior where no relationship or commitment exists;
- references native Project, Workspace, Discipline, User, Context, and source
  identities instead of copying them.

## Relationship Identity Review

**PASS WITH MINOR CORRECTION REQUIRED**

Relationship identity is explicit, stable, traceable, Project-scoped,
direction-aware, versioned, lifecycle-preserving, and resistant to accidental
active duplication.

Conceptual uniqueness includes:

- governing Project;
- relationship meaning;
- direction;
- source endpoint;
- target endpoint;
- bounded purpose or applicability.

The IDS rejects generic custom labels and generic relationship payloads.
Identity is never inferred.

The future validation matrix should explicitly require rejection of prohibited
self-reference. The current endpoint validation covers missing, inaccessible,
cross-Project, and out-of-scope endpoints but does not name self-reference.

## Relationship-Type Review

**PASS**

The relationship vocabulary is allow-listed rather than free-form. Each
initial meaning is directional and explainable. Meaning conversion is
prohibited as an ordinary update, preventing inverse or semantically different
relationships from being silently collapsed.

Potential later EDS meanings such as derived from, conflicts with, supersedes,
corrects, reviews, decides, snapshot of, and historical precedent for are not
pulled into this patch.

No implementation enum or storage representation is defined.

## Interface Commitment Review

**PASS**

Interface Commitment is clearly distinguished from a Context Relationship and
from workflow or task tracking.

It conceptually includes:

- stable identity and one governing Project;
- authorized provider;
- one consuming Workspace;
- required engineering information;
- stated use and completeness expectations;
- source and revision expectations;
- stage or due condition without schedule execution;
- recorded criticality standing without assessment automation;
- steward and consumer review responsibility;
- confidentiality;
- lifecycle and change-impact standing;
- optimistic concurrency and traceability.

Provider and consumer accountability is explicit. Commitments cannot be
silently fulfilled. Provider delivery does not mean consumer acceptance or
engineering approval. Human Review evidence may be required for fulfilment,
but this patch cannot create it.

Rejected, disputed, and superseded commitments preserve identity. The minor
withdrawal clarification identified under EDS alignment is still required.

## Lifecycle Review

**PASS WITH MINOR CORRECTION REQUIRED**

Relationship lifecycle is separate from:

- Interface Commitment lifecycle;
- Context lifecycle;
- Workspace lifecycle;
- Project status;
- Human Review state.

Relationship withdrawal and restoration preserve identity and require reason,
authorization, and expected version. Interface Commitment states carry
information-delivery meaning rather than task progress.

No lifecycle silently changes Context authority, creates dependent records,
erases identity, or invokes workflow.

The Interface Commitment validation section must explicitly resolve
withdrawal semantics and cover the resulting permitted and prohibited
transitions. This is a bounded lifecycle clarification, not authorization to
add history or workflow.

## Authorization Review

**PASS**

The IDS preserves:

- authoritative Project scope;
- bounded Workspace participation;
- same-Project-only cross-Workspace relationships;
- independent provider and consumer visibility;
- protected identifier non-disclosure;
- source-confidentiality enforcement;
- authorization before disclosure, counts, pagination, traversal, or mutation;
- current `admin` and `engineer` RBAC only.

Cross-Project relationships are prohibited. Because every relationship has one
governing Project and every Workspace endpoint must belong to it,
cross-Customer relationship linkage cannot bypass the Project boundary.

Relationship visibility grants no transitive access. Administrative access
does not automatically bypass restricted source meaning or confer technical
authority.

## Authority and Responsibility Review

**PASS**

The IDS keeps:

- ownership separate from engineering competence;
- provider responsibility separate from formal approval;
- consumer response separate from Context authority;
- relationship existence separate from technical validity;
- fulfilment dependent on explicit governed evidence;
- authority separate from confidence, maturity, lifecycle, review, and
  criticality.

Provider, consumer, information owner, steward, and reviewer references remain
distinct. Responsibility changes are explicit, authorized, version-protected,
and audited.

## Concurrency Review

**PASS WITH MINOR CORRECTION REQUIRED**

Optimistic concurrency covers material relationship and commitment mutation,
lifecycle transition, responsibility change, supplied-revision change, stale
rejection, one-winner behavior, and unchanged audit state after conflict.

The validation contract must additionally name:

- provider change where permitted;
- consumer change where permitted;
- stale commitment fulfilment;
- commitment withdrawal after withdrawal semantics are clarified.

These are required matrix cases already implied by the general mutation rule,
but they must be explicit and measurable before implementation planning.

## Audit Review

**PASS WITH MINOR CORRECTION REQUIRED**

Audit and business mutation are required to complete atomically. The IDS
requires no success audit for validation, authorization, integrity,
lifecycle, or concurrency rejection and requires rollback when audit
persistence fails.

The success-event list must explicitly add, where permitted:

- relationship metadata updated;
- provider changed;
- consumer changed;
- commitment withdrawn after withdrawal semantics are clarified;
- criticality standing changed;
- source relationship changed.

The audit evidence contract already requires before and after standing, actor,
reason, scope, references, version, and timestamp.

## Migration and PostgreSQL Review

**PASS WITH MINOR CORRECTION REQUIRED**

The IDS requires additive planning, isolated PostgreSQL validation, exact
database-name guards, forward application, rollback and recovery,
reapplication, prior-head compatibility, metadata comparison, direct
constraint rejection, and development-database fingerprint protection.

It defines no SQL, table name, constraint name, index name, or revision ID.

The validation obligations must explicitly add:

- deterministic rollback;
- fresh-chain migration validation;
- compatibility from the current repository head;
- restrictive foreign-key behavior where history protection requires it;
- prohibition of existing Context mutation or backfill unless separately
  approved.

These are delivery safeguards, not persistence design.

## Validation Completeness

**PASS WITH MINOR CORRECTIONS REQUIRED**

The IDS provides broad, measurable coverage for identity, relationship meaning,
scope, lifecycle, commitment states, authority, permissions, cross-Workspace
access, reassessment, concurrency, audit rollback, PostgreSQL integrity,
compatibility, regression, performance, security, and scope exclusions.

The future test contract must explicitly add:

- relationship retrieval;
- permitted relationship metadata update;
- prohibited self-reference;
- commitment withdrawal;
- provider and consumer change where permitted;
- stale commitment fulfilment;
- stale commitment withdrawal;
- audit coverage for each missing success event;
- restrictive foreign-key history protection;
- fresh-chain migration replay;
- OpenAPI generation regression.

Universal Search regression is already covered by the complete backend
regression requirement but should be named explicitly alongside the other
compatibility suites because Search implementation itself remains excluded.

## Performance Coverage

**PASS**

The IDS requires the implementation plan to define a deterministic,
representative same-Project cross-Workspace dataset and approved thresholds.
It identifies:

- relationship detail;
- bounded relationship listing;
- Interface Commitment detail;
- provider and consumer scoped listing;
- lifecycle mutation;
- stale-version rejection.

It also requires security to be applied before disclosure, counts, pagination,
or traversal and prohibits performance optimization from weakening
confidentiality, traceability, audit, or concurrency.

## Blocking Issues

None.

The identified corrections do not require an ADR or EDS change and do not
alter the approved domain model.

## Minor Corrections

Before formal IDS acceptance:

1. Clarify governed Interface Commitment withdrawal semantics without changing
   the accepted EDS state model or introducing workflow/history behavior.
2. Expand concurrency validation to name provider and consumer changes,
   commitment fulfilment, and commitment withdrawal.
3. Expand audit success events to name relationship metadata update,
   provider/consumer change, commitment withdrawal, criticality-standing
   change, and source-relationship change.
4. Expand migration obligations to require deterministic rollback,
   fresh-chain validation, current-head compatibility, restrictive
   history-protection foreign keys, and no existing Context backfill or
   mutation without separate approval.
5. Expand explicit tests for retrieval, permitted metadata update,
   self-reference rejection, withdrawal, the missing stale-mutation cases,
   missing audit events, foreign-key history protection, fresh-chain replay,
   Universal Search regression, and OpenAPI generation.
6. Name ENSE and Engineering Memory explicitly in non-scope for defensive
   completeness.

## Non-Blocking Risks

- Relationship meaning may drift toward a generic graph if later patches
  bypass the allow-list.
- Interface Commitment lifecycle may drift toward task workflow unless actor
  and state semantics remain strictly enforced.
- Cross-Workspace performance optimization may accidentally introduce
  transitive disclosure.
- Provider source revisions may be treated as harmless unless reassessment
  evidence remains visible.
- Future Human Review integration may blur delivery and approval unless the
  current separation is retained.
- Preserved disputed and superseded standing will require careful later
  historical and Conflict-domain integration.

These risks are already recognized by ADR-015 and EDS-020.2 and are not
acceptance blockers once the explicit validation corrections are made.

## IDS Readiness

The IDS is architecturally complete and implementation-independent. It is not
yet ready to be marked Accepted because the required validation contract needs
the bounded corrections listed above.

After approved correction and focused re-review, the IDS can proceed to formal
acceptance and subsequent implementation planning. Implementation remains
prohibited.
