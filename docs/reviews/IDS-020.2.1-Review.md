## Scope Verdict

**PASS**

The IDS defines a minimum viable Core Context Foundation:

- stable Context identity;
- explicit Project or Workspace scope;
- Subject Reference, Qualified Fact, Qualified Engineering Value, Assumption,
  and Source and Evidence Reference kinds;
- information ownership and engineering stewardship;
- bounded authority and source foundations;
- only the subject, scope, and source relationships required by the core;
- current-use, withdrawal, version, and optimistic concurrency behavior;
- authorization and confidentiality;
- baseline audit integration;
- PATCH-020.1 compatibility.

The minimum relationship meanings are expressly distinguished from the general
relationship capability assigned to PATCH-020.2.2. The IDS does not authorize
later PATCH-020.2 capabilities.

## Non-Scope Verdict

**PASS**

The IDS expressly excludes:

- AI behavior, AI Insights, and AI-generated Context;
- Derived Context;
- Missing Information and Conflict engines;
- Historical Context and snapshots;
- Context search;
- Engineering Decision Log;
- Engineering Execution Plan;
- Engineering Health and Workspace Readiness;
- Knowledge Graph;
- Interface Commitments and general relationship traversal;
- source-precedence resolution;
- Human Review and authority-promotion workflows;
- formal approval;
- later maturity, freshness, criticality, correction, and supersession
  capabilities.

The forbidden-behavior section reinforces these exclusions and prohibits
implementation outside the PATCH, EDS, and IDS boundary.

## ADR-015 Alignment

**PASS**

The IDS preserves ADR-015’s core decisions:

- Engineering Context is governed and multidimensional rather than a generic
  blob;
- no universal Engineering Context Object erases native semantics;
- native Project, Workspace, Discipline, User, source, and engineering-subject
  identities remain authoritative;
- Context scope, source, authority, ownership, and responsibility remain
  explicit;
- Project-shared meaning is not duplicated into Workspaces;
- Workspace-local meaning remains attributable to its Workspace;
- values preserve the qualifications necessary for engineering meaning;
- ownership, stewardship, competence, authorization, and authority remain
  distinct;
- relationships do not transfer ownership, authority, maturity, competence, or
  approval;
- unsupported and concurrent changes fail safely.

The IDS does not weaken Human Review or AI safety boundaries.

## EDS-020.2 Alignment

**PASS WITH ONE MINOR REQUIRED CORRECTION**

The IDS is a strict functional subset of PATCH-020.2.1 as ordered by accepted
EDS-020.2. It covers the required core taxonomy, Project and Workspace scope,
qualified facts and engineering values, assumptions, responsibility,
dimensional standing, concurrency, audit, authorization, and Workspace Core
compatibility.

It correctly defers PATCH-020.2.2 through PATCH-020.2.8.

One readiness-evidence omission requires correction before IRR: the IDS
excludes persisted-state transition work and does not explicitly require
migration validation and direct PostgreSQL constraint evidence. EDS-020.2
requires separately authorized data design and isolated database validation,
and the Development Lifecycle requires an IDS to state applicable migration,
rollback, and database-specific validation obligations.

This does not require technical design in the IDS. The IDS need only establish
the applicable obligation and measurable evidence; detailed implementation
design remains deferred.

## Workspace Core Compatibility

**PASS**

The IDS preserves:

- one Workspace identity per Project and Discipline;
- derived Workspace naming;
- existing Workspace owner, primary assignee, and collaborator semantics;
- Workspace lifecycle, archival state, concurrency, permission, and audit
  behavior;
- Project ownership of Customer and Project lifecycle;
- native Project and Workspace identity;
- supported Project and Workspace behavior when no Context exists.

It prohibits Context mutation from changing Workspace or Project identity,
ownership, assignment, collaboration, Discipline, lifecycle, or archival
state.

## Authority and Ownership Review

**PASS**

The IDS separates:

- information ownership;
- engineering stewardship;
- authorization;
- professional competence;
- authority standing;
- administrator access.

It states that ownership, assignment, collaboration, administration,
stewardship, recency, repetition, confidence, and source presence cannot
silently establish competence or promote authority.

Authority remains bounded to purpose and scope. Assumptions remain provisional
and distinct from facts. Human Review and authority-promotion workflows remain
out of scope.

The Engineer-Verified standing is limited to preservation of standing already
supported by qualifying human evidence and authorization. Implementation
planning must preserve that narrow boundary and must not introduce a
verification action through PATCH-020.2.1.

## Source and Traceability Review

**PASS**

Source references retain:

- native source identity;
- source kind and owner;
- revision or issue;
- effective or observation time;
- source-supplied standing;
- confidentiality;
- applicability;
- known limitations.

The source remains a native object. Context does not copy, own, approve, or
replace it. The IDS prohibits universal source precedence, automated source
interpretation, silent conflict resolution, and disclosure beyond source
authorization.

Traceability covers Context identity, Project and Workspace scope, subjects,
responsible actors, sources, material before-and-after meaning, concurrency
basis, reason, and time.

## Lifecycle and Concurrency Review

**PASS**

The minimum lifecycle distinguishes current use from explicit withdrawal.
Withdrawal requires authorization and reason and does not delete the native
source or subject. Full history, correction, supersession, expiry, and snapshot
behavior remain deferred.

Optimistic concurrency is behaviorally complete:

- every material change requires a current concurrency basis;
- each successful material change advances it exactly once;
- stale changes are rejected atomically;
- rejected changes do not partially alter Context or links;
- the prior valid state and concurrency basis remain intact.

The IDS avoids prescribing a technology-specific concurrency representation.

## Authorization Review

**PASS**

Authorization uses existing roles together with Project and Workspace
ownership, assignment, collaboration, information ownership, stewardship, and
source restrictions. It creates no new role.

The IDS prevents:

- cross-Project links;
- inaccessible subject or source links;
- unrestricted cross-Workspace traversal;
- participation from becoming verification authority;
- administrator access from becoming technical competence;
- Project authority from silently becoming Workspace stewardship;
- source disclosure through Context participation.

Because general cross-Workspace relationships are out of scope, the remaining
minimum links are constrained to the governing Project, its Workspace where
applicable, native subjects, and authorized sources.

## Audit Rollback Review

**PASS**

Successful material creation, value change, source-link change,
responsibility change, authority-standing change, and lifecycle change require
centralized audit evidence.

The IDS explicitly requires no success audit evidence for:

- invalid identity or scope;
- unsupported Context kind;
- unauthorized access;
- inaccessible source or subject;
- invalid responsibility;
- missing engineering qualification;
- unsupported authority;
- prohibited relationship;
- stale concurrency basis;
- rejected lifecycle action;
- partial attempted change.

Context change and its success audit evidence must complete together. Rejected
behavior preserves prior Context meaning, links, concurrency basis, and audit
state.

## Validation Completeness

**PASS WITH ONE MINOR REQUIRED CORRECTION**

The validation plan is measurable and covers:

- all allowed and unsupported Context kinds;
- Project and Workspace scoping;
- qualified values and Assumptions;
- source and minimum relationship rules;
- complete actor and responsibility boundaries;
- authority standing and prohibited promotion;
- optimistic concurrency;
- audit success and rollback behavior;
- Workspace Core compatibility;
- direct integrity rejection;
- focused and complete regression;
- syntax, static integrity, formatting, cache cleanup, working-tree scope, and
  development-data fingerprint;
- performance and security considerations for IRR.

Required correction:

1. Clarify that persisted-state transition work is applicable implementation
   scope when needed to realize Core Context persistence; only premature
   technical design is excluded from the IDS phase.
2. Require deterministic forward transition and rollback or recovery evidence
   in the isolated validation environment.
3. Require direct PostgreSQL constraint validation for the integrity rules
   selected by the later approved Implementation Plan.
4. Require confirmation that protected development data remains unchanged.

The correction must remain behavioral. It must not define SQL, table names,
constraint names, revision identifiers, or storage representation.

## Implementation Leakage Review

**PASS**

The IDS does not define:

- SQL;
- database table or column names;
- endpoint paths or transport contracts;
- Pydantic schemas;
- repository classes;
- service methods;
- migration revision identifiers;
- implementation filenames;
- framework, library, or technology-specific representations.

Its domain responsibilities, observable behaviors, integrity expectations, and
validation criteria are appropriate bounded implementation expectations.
Detailed implementation design remains deferred to the Implementation Plan
after IDS approval.

## Blocking Issues

No architectural or scope-blocking issue was found.

The validation omission identified above is a minor required correction.
Although it does not require EDS or ADR change, the IDS must not advance to IRR
until that correction is approved and applied.

## Non-Blocking Risks

1. **Initial source and subject availability:** EDS-020.2 leaves the exact
   governed native reference inventory to sub-patch evidence. IRR should
   confirm the supported initial inventory without broadening the IDS.
2. **Engineer-Verified standing boundary:** Implementation could accidentally
   introduce verification behavior assigned to PATCH-020.2.3. The
   Implementation Plan must limit this patch to preserving qualifying existing
   standing.
3. **Relationship expansion:** Minimum subject, scope, and source links could
   drift into general traversal. Tests must enforce the relationship
   allow-list.
4. **Qualified-value completeness:** Required qualifications vary by
   engineering meaning. The Implementation Plan must define deterministic
   accepted cases without inventing a universal measurement model.
5. **Representative scale:** Performance targets and the isolated dataset
   remain to be approved at IRR.

## IDS Readiness

The IDS is architecturally coherent, appropriately bounded, measurable, and
free of premature implementation design.

It is ready for one focused minor correction. It is not yet ready to receive
**Ready for Implementation** because migration and direct PostgreSQL
constraint validation obligations are incomplete.

## Recommendation

**Accept with minor corrections**

## Final Verdict

**ACCEPT WITH MINOR CORRECTIONS**

The IDS may be accepted after the single validation-readiness correction is
approved and applied. No ADR, EDS, or PATCH scope change is required. The IDS
must then receive focused re-review before Implementation Readiness Review.
