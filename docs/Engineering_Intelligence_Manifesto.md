## 1. The Vision

Engineering work depends on the ability to understand a changing body of
requirements, evidence, decisions, relationships, and consequences. That body
of knowledge is larger than any individual deliverable and persists beyond the
people, projects, and systems that first produced it. SATCO exists to make this
engineering context durable, comprehensible, and available to those who carry
responsibility for the work.

The long-term vision is an engineering environment in which relevant knowledge
can be understood at the point of decision. Engineers should be able to see
what is known, what is uncertain, what has changed, what is connected, and what
requires review without reconstructing the history of the work from fragmented
records. The purpose is not to remove complexity, but to make necessary
complexity visible and navigable.

SATCO advances organizational capability by preserving the relationship
between engineering work and the reasoning that shaped it. Knowledge remains
useful when its context, provenance, responsibility, and approval state remain
intact. As work evolves, the platform must support continued understanding
without silently rewriting prior facts or decisions.

This vision places accountable engineers at the center. SATCO may strengthen
analysis, continuity, and awareness, but Engineering Judgment remains a Human
responsibility. Assistance has value only when it enables informed review and
preserves the authority of the people responsible for engineering outcomes.

> **Foundation Statement:** SATCO shall make governed engineering context a durable organizational capability while preserving Human responsibility for every engineering decision.

## 2. Engineering Intelligence Definition

Engineering Intelligence is the governed capability to transform connected
engineering context into understanding that supports accountable action. It
brings together the relevant facts, relationships, evidence, history,
constraints, and approval state required to interpret Engineering Work without
detaching conclusions from their basis.

Engineering Intelligence is not the possession of information alone. It exists
when information can be interpreted within the scope in which it is valid,
when dependencies and consequences can be examined, and when uncertainty and
missing knowledge remain visible. Its value is measured by improved
understanding, stronger review, earlier recognition of risk, and better
continuity of engineering responsibility.

Engineering Intelligence distinguishes observation from interpretation,
recommendation from decision, and recorded fact from unresolved assumption.
It preserves the path from an engineering question to the context considered
and the Human approval required. A result that cannot be traced, reviewed, or
placed within its governing context is not authoritative Engineering
Intelligence.

The capability is organizational rather than personal. It must remain useful
across disciplines, project stages, changes in personnel, and changes in the
tools or providers used to assist the work. Its continuity depends on governed
knowledge and explicit responsibility, not on any individual system response.

> **Foundation Statement:** Engineering Intelligence is governed, traceable understanding that strengthens Engineering Judgment without becoming engineering authority.

## 3. The Engineering Problem

Engineering organizations produce substantial information, yet often lack a
reliable way to preserve the context needed to understand it. Requirements,
calculations, correspondence, reviews, decisions, revisions, and field
observations are distributed across documents, applications, disciplines, and
individual experience. Each record may be valid in isolation while the meaning
of the whole remains difficult to recover.

The central problem is discontinuity. Engineering Work evolves through
interdependent decisions made at different times by different people. When the
relationship between a decision, its evidence, its assumptions, and its
affected work is not preserved, later teams must reconstruct intent. That
reconstruction consumes time, introduces uncertainty, and can conceal risk.

Information loss also occurs without deletion. Context is lost when a revision
is separated from its reason, when an approval is separated from its scope,
when a requirement is separated from its source, or when a lesson is reused
without the conditions that made it valid. The record may remain available
while its engineering meaning has become incomplete.

This problem compounds as organizations grow. More projects, disciplines, and
participants increase the number of dependencies that must be understood.
Individual expertise remains essential, but it cannot by itself provide
consistent organizational memory or continuity. The organization requires a
governed means to retain understanding as Engineering Work and professional
responsibility move across time and boundaries.

> **Foundation Statement:** Engineering information becomes organizational knowledge only when its context, relationships, rationale, and authority remain recoverable.

## 4. Why Traditional Knowledge Management Fails

Traditional Knowledge Management treats knowledge as material to be collected
after work has occurred. Documents are stored, categorized, and retrieved, but
the engineering circumstances that gave them meaning are often absent. The
result is an archive of outputs rather than a reliable account of Engineering
Work.

Documentation is commonly separated from the activity it describes. Decisions
are recorded without the alternatives considered, lessons are summarized
without the conditions under which they were learned, and approvals are retained
without the precise scope to which they applied. This separation weakens the
connection between an engineering record and the judgment that produced it.

Traditional systems also depend on deliberate contribution as a separate task.
Engineers must decide what to document, where to place it, how to classify it,
and whether it may be useful later. Under delivery pressure, that additional
work is incomplete or deferred, leaving important Engineering Experience in
personal notes, correspondence, conversations, and individual recollection.

Retrieval does not resolve the problem. Finding a document does not establish
whether it remains applicable, what has changed since it was approved, or how
its conclusions relate to the current situation. Access to information is not
the same as understanding its engineering significance.

Engineering Intelligence therefore requires a different philosophy: knowledge
must remain connected to the work, context, responsibility, and decisions from
which it emerged. Preservation cannot begin only after engineering is complete;
it must be inherent in how the organization understands Engineering Work.

> **Foundation Statement:** Engineering knowledge cannot be preserved reliably when documentation is separated from the work, context, and judgment that created it.

## 5. SATCO Philosophy

SATCO treats Engineering Intelligence as a permanent organizational capability,
not as a feature, repository, or temporary initiative. The capability belongs
to the engineering organization and must endure across projects, disciplines,
personnel changes, and the continuing evolution of its tools.

Engineering Context Is Sacred. Facts, assumptions, evidence, decisions,
responsibilities, and approvals derive meaning from the circumstances in which
they apply. SATCO shall preserve those circumstances and shall not detach an
engineering conclusion from the boundaries that make it valid.

Intelligence Before Automation. SATCO shall first improve understanding,
traceability, and the quality of Engineering Judgment. Automation is acceptable
only when it preserves Human responsibility and does not obscure uncertainty,
rationale, or the need for approval.

Engineering Experience is valuable when it can inform new work without being
mistaken for a universal answer. Prior outcomes must remain connected to their
original constraints so that engineers can evaluate relevance rather than
inherit conclusions without review.

Continuous Evolution must remain governed. Organizational capability should
grow as Engineering Work produces validated understanding, while prior records
retain their meaning and authority. Improvement shall add context and clarity;
it shall not silently revise engineering history.

> **Foundation Statement:** SATCO shall develop Engineering Intelligence as a governed organizational capability founded on preserved context, Human authority, and continuous responsible learning.

## 6. Capture Once Architecture

Capture Once Architecture establishes that Engineering Work should be recorded
at its point of origin and should not require repeated manual reconstruction for
each later use. A captured engineering fact, observation, rationale, or decision
remains a single governed record whose meaning can be carried forward.

Capture once does not mean describe once for every purpose. It means that later
understanding begins from the original record rather than from copied,
re-entered, or paraphrased versions. Different uses may present or interpret the
record within their authorized scope while preserving its identity and source.

Everything after capture is enrichment. Engineering context may be clarified,
relationships may be recognized, evidence may be associated, and later
developments may add interpretation. Enrichment supplements the original
record; it does not overwrite its provenance, time, responsibility, or approval
state.

Correction is distinct from erasure. When Engineering Work changes, the change
must remain understandable in relation to what preceded it. The organization
must be able to distinguish the original capture, subsequent enrichment, and
approved correction without presenting them as one undifferentiated statement.

The principle reduces avoidable recapture while protecting engineering meaning.
Its purpose is not administrative efficiency alone, but continuity: engineers
should be able to rely on the same preserved context as knowledge moves across
disciplines, project stages, and future use.

> **Foundation Statement:** Engineering Work shall be captured once at its source and thereafter enriched without losing its original context, provenance, or authority.

## 7. Engineering Intelligence Transformation Lifecycle

Engineering Intelligence develops through a continuous transformation
lifecycle. Engineering Work produces experience; experience is captured with
its original context; context is enriched through relationships and evidence;
meaning is formed through interpretation; and accountable Human Approval
determines what the organization may trust and reuse.

The lifecycle begins in the work itself. An observation, question, decision,
exception, outcome, or lesson has value because it arises within a specific
engineering situation. Its initial meaning depends on who was responsible,
what was known, what constraints applied, and what action followed.

Captured experience becomes more useful as its context is preserved and
clarified. Relevant scope, dependencies, sources, assumptions, and changes make
the experience understandable beyond the moment and the individuals involved.
This transformation adds meaning without changing the original record.

Interpretation turns contextualized experience into a potential insight.
Patterns may become visible, consequences may be recognized, and prior work may
inform a current question. Interpretation remains provisional until qualified
people evaluate its relevance, limitations, and engineering significance.

Approved understanding can then support later Engineering Work. Its use creates
new experience, including confirmation, correction, exception, or a different
outcome under different conditions. That experience returns to the lifecycle,
allowing organizational capability to evolve without treating prior knowledge
as universally or permanently applicable.

The lifecycle is therefore not a one-time movement from work into storage. It
is a governed cycle in which application produces further evidence and every
new outcome can deepen, qualify, or challenge what the organization previously
understood.

> **Foundation Statement:** Engineering Intelligence shall evolve through a continuous governed lifecycle that transforms Engineering Experience into reviewed understanding and returns learning to future Engineering Work.

## 8. Engineering Journal & Universal Capture

The Engineering Journal is the natural workspace in which Engineering
Experience is captured as part of Engineering Work. It preserves observations,
questions, rationale, assumptions, decisions, outcomes, and lessons in the
context in which they arise. The Journal is not a retrospective reporting
obligation or a separate knowledge-management exercise.

Engineers should not have to interrupt responsible work to translate their
experience into a second documentation process. Capture should follow the
natural expression of engineering activity while preserving the distinction
between an informal observation, a working interpretation, and an approved
engineering record.

This principle defines the relationship between engineering and documentation:

> The engineer should never ask:
> 'How do I document this?'
>
> The only question should be:
> 'How do I perform good engineering?'
>
> SATCO is responsible for everything that follows.

Universal Capture establishes one trusted entry point for Engineering
Experience regardless of its source or form. An experience may originate in
direct work, discussion, review, correspondence, field activity, an existing
record, or assisted analysis. Its origin remains visible, while the act of
bringing it into governed context remains consistent.

Universal does not mean indiscriminate. Captured material retains its source,
scope, responsibility, confidentiality, and status. Capture makes experience
available for governed transformation; it does not make every statement true,
approved, or suitable for reuse.

The Engineering Journal and Universal Capture together realize Capture Once
Architecture at the point of work. They allow later enrichment to begin from a
preserved source rather than requiring engineers to recreate meaning for each
new audience or purpose.

> **Foundation Statement:** Engineering Experience shall be captured naturally within Engineering Work through one trusted entry point, without imposing a separate documentation activity on the engineer.

## 9. Engineering Intelligence Pipeline

The Engineering Intelligence Pipeline is the permanent transformation process
through which captured Engineering Experience becomes trusted organizational
capability. It is defined by the progression of engineering meaning, not by a
particular tool, provider, or technical arrangement.

The first stage preserves the experience as expressed at its source. It retains
provenance, time, responsibility, scope, and original form so that later
understanding can be traced to what actually occurred.

The second stage establishes context. The experience is placed within the
Project, Workspace, discipline, lifecycle, constraints, and authority in which
it has meaning. Context limits interpretation as well as enabling it.

The third stage connects relevant knowledge. Relationships to engineering
objects, evidence, requirements, decisions, changes, and prior experience make
dependencies and possible consequences visible without asserting a conclusion.

The fourth stage develops interpretation. Observations can be compared,
inconsistencies identified, implications considered, and potential lessons
expressed. Each interpretation must preserve the distinction between known
facts, assumptions, uncertainty, and recommendation.

The fifth stage subjects the interpretation to accountable review. Engineering
Judgment determines whether the understanding is valid, under what conditions
it may be relied upon, and what approval state it may receive. No prior stage
grants engineering authority.

The final stage makes reviewed understanding available for responsible use.
Application to later work remains contextual and may produce new evidence or a
different conclusion. The Pipeline therefore remains active as the
organization learns from use, change, and outcome.

Every stage must increase engineering understanding. Movement through the
Pipeline is not justified by processing volume or completion of an
administrative sequence; it is justified only when context, traceability,
meaning, or confidence has been strengthened without obscuring uncertainty.

> **Foundation Statement:** The Engineering Intelligence Pipeline shall transform captured experience into trusted organizational capability by increasing context, connection, interpretation, review, and responsible reuse at every stage.

## 10. Engineering Intelligence Author

The Engineering Intelligence Author is the conceptual capability that
transforms captured Engineering Experience into structured engineering drafts.
It organizes relevant context, evidence, relationships, assumptions, and
uncertainty so that an engineer can examine a coherent expression of the
subject under consideration.

The Author assists engineering thinking by making reasoning explicit. It may
frame a question, organize observations, identify gaps, compare relevant
experience, or express a proposed interpretation. Its purpose is to help the
engineer review meaning, not to produce an authoritative conclusion.

Every draft must remain distinguishable from an approved engineering record.
Its sources, scope, assumptions, and limitations must be available for
examination, and its language must not imply authority that has not been
granted. The quality of expression cannot substitute for the quality of
engineering basis.

The Author never replaces Engineering Judgment. It cannot accept professional
responsibility, determine that evidence is sufficient, resolve an engineering
disagreement, or approve the use of its own work. Those responsibilities remain
with qualified people.

The capability must remain independent of any provider used to assist it.
Providers may contribute analytical or generative capacity, but no provider
owns the engineering context, determines the governing meaning, or becomes the
authority for the resulting draft. SATCO preserves continuity when providers
change.

> **Foundation Statement:** The Engineering Intelligence Author shall create traceable engineering drafts that assist Human reasoning while never assuming Engineering Judgment or authority.

## 11. Human Review & Approval

Human Review is the permanent trust boundary of the SATCO Platform. It is the
point at which a qualified person evaluates whether a proposed understanding is
supported, relevant, sufficiently complete, and suitable for its stated
engineering purpose.

AI assistance and engineering authority are categorically different. AI may
organize, analyze, compare, explain, and recommend; it cannot determine that an
engineering conclusion is accepted. Fluency, confidence, repetition, or prior
successful use does not grant authority to an assisted result.

Engineering Intelligence does not become organizational capability without
explicit engineer approval. Approval identifies the accountable person, the
scope of acceptance, the context considered, and any conditions or limitations
that remain. Silence, use, elapsed time, or system processing is not approval.

Human Review is not merely a workflow step placed after analysis. It is the
foundation of organizational trust because it connects knowledge to
professional responsibility. Review may confirm, qualify, reject, return, or
supersede a proposed understanding, and each outcome must remain visible.

Approval does not make knowledge universal or permanent. An approved conclusion
remains bounded by its evidence, assumptions, scope, and time. Later change may
require renewed review, but it does not erase the authority or rationale of the
earlier decision within its original context.

> **Foundation Statement:** No Engineering Intelligence shall enter trusted organizational use without explicit, accountable, and context-bounded Human Approval.

## 12. Engineering Organizational Memory

Engineering Organizational Memory is the continuously evolving capability to
retain and responsibly reuse approved Engineering Experience. It preserves not
only conclusions, but also the context, evidence, rationale, responsibility,
conditions, and outcomes required to understand what the organization learned.

Engineering knowledge belongs to the organization within its governed scope.
It does not belong exclusively to the individual who first understood it, the
team that first applied it, or the software system that helped express it.
People and systems may change while the organization's accountable knowledge
must remain durable.

Organizational Memory is selective rather than accumulative. Captured material
does not become trusted memory merely because it exists. Human Approval
determines what may be relied upon, while rejected, provisional, and superseded
material remains distinguishable as part of the record rather than being
presented as current knowledge.

Collective Engineering Intelligence emerges when approved experience can
strengthen understanding beyond its original participants. It allows one
discipline, Project, or generation of engineers to learn from another while
preserving the differences that govern whether prior knowledge is relevant.

Every approved engineering experience can strengthen future decisions across
the organization. Its later use may confirm the existing understanding, expose
an exception, or produce a more complete conclusion. Each outcome contributes
to Continuous Evolution without silently changing the history on which prior
decisions depended.

The strength of Organizational Memory is therefore measured by continuity and
responsible applicability, not by the volume of retained information. Memory
serves Engineering Judgment when engineers can understand both what was learned
and the boundaries within which that learning may be trusted.

> **Foundation Statement:** Approved Engineering Experience shall become durable Organizational Memory that builds Collective Engineering Intelligence while preserving context, authority, and responsible reuse.

## 13. Engineering Knowledge Graph

The Engineering Knowledge Graph is the conceptual model through which SATCO
connects engineering knowledge. It represents Engineering Work as a coherent
body of meaning in which identity, context, evidence, responsibility, history,
and consequence can be understood together.

The Graph does not derive its value from storing more information. Its purpose
is to preserve the relationships that explain why information matters: what an
experience concerns, which Engineering Object is affected, what evidence
supports a conclusion, which standard governs it, what decision followed, and
where that decision applies.

Engineering Intelligence grows as relationships connect Engineering
Experience, Engineering Objects, evidence, standards, decisions, Projects, and
Engineering Organizational Memory. These connections allow engineers to
examine dependencies, trace rationale, recognize impact, and evaluate whether
prior understanding is relevant to present work.

Relationships must be governed with the same care as the knowledge they
connect. A relationship expresses an engineering assertion and therefore
requires a defined meaning, valid scope, traceable basis, and appropriate
authority. An unqualified connection can mislead as readily as an unsupported
statement.

The quality of the Graph is determined by the quality of its relationships,
not by the quantity of its records. A smaller body of well-defined, contextual,
and reviewable connections provides stronger Engineering Intelligence than a
large collection whose relevance and authority cannot be established.

The Graph remains open to governed extension as Engineering Work evolves. New
disciplines and organizational capabilities may contribute knowledge through
explicit meanings and boundaries without weakening the shared principles that
make existing knowledge understandable.

> **Foundation Statement:** The Engineering Knowledge Graph shall create engineering meaning through governed, contextual, and traceable relationships rather than through the accumulation of information alone.

## 14. AI Provider Independence

AI Provider Independence is a permanent architectural principle of SATCO.
No provider, model, or generation of technology may become the owner or
authority of the organization's engineering knowledge.

Artificial Intelligence is an implementation capability. It may assist with
analysis, comparison, expression, retrieval, or explanation, but its role is
bounded by the Engineering Context, governance, and Human Approval that SATCO
preserves independently of it.

Engineering Intelligence is a permanent organizational capability. It resides
in governed Engineering Experience, relationships, evidence, decisions, and
Organizational Memory. Its continuity depends on retained meaning and
responsibility, not on the continued availability or behavior of a particular
AI service.

Providers, models, and technologies may change as capabilities and obligations
evolve. Such change must not break the organization's ability to understand
prior engineering work, trace approved conclusions, or continue responsible
use of its knowledge.

Provider output never becomes authoritative by origin. It remains subject to
the same requirements for evidence, explainability, uncertainty, scope, and
Human Review as any other assisted interpretation.

Long-term ownership remains with the organization. SATCO must preserve the
freedom to select, replace, combine, or discontinue providers while retaining
control of engineering context and the accumulated capability derived from it.

> **Foundation Statement:** AI providers may change, but Engineering Intelligence, engineering knowledge, and engineering authority shall remain governed and owned by the organization.

## 15. Non-Negotiable Principles

The following principles govern every future SATCO architecture and
implementation decision. They are permanent decision boundaries and may not be
weakened by convenience, technology choice, delivery pressure, or apparent
automation benefit.

1. **Engineering First.** Every capability shall serve a real engineering need
   and strengthen understanding, responsibility, decision quality, risk
   control, readiness, or organizational learning.

2. **Capture Once.** Engineering Work shall be captured at its source and
   enriched thereafter without repeated reconstruction or loss of original
   meaning.

3. **Human Authority.** Engineering Judgment, professional responsibility, and
   approval shall remain with qualified people; assisted output shall never
   grant itself authority.

4. **Engineering Context Is Sacred.** Engineering knowledge shall remain bound
   to the scope, conditions, provenance, responsibility, and time that determine
   its meaning.

5. **Evidence Before Assumption.** Known facts and supporting evidence shall be
   distinguished from inference, uncertainty, and missing information; an
   assumption shall never be presented as an engineering fact.

6. **Context Before Recommendation.** No recommendation shall be treated as
   meaningful until the relevant engineering situation and the boundaries of
   applicability are understood.

7. **Intelligence Before Automation.** SATCO shall improve understanding and
   trust before automating action; automation shall never conceal uncertainty
   or bypass accountable review.

8. **Explainability.** Every material assisted interpretation shall be capable
   of showing its basis, assumptions, limitations, and required Human Review.

9. **Provider Independence.** No provider, model, or technology shall control
   SATCO's engineering meaning, continuity, or authority.

10. **Organizational Ownership.** Governed engineering knowledge shall belong
    to the organization within its authorized scope, not exclusively to an
    individual, team, or software system.

11. **Continuous Evolution.** Approved knowledge may be extended, qualified,
    corrected, or superseded through governance while prior context, rationale,
    and decision history remain intact.

These principles operate together. A decision that satisfies one principle by
violating another is not consistent with this Manifesto. Where tension exists,
the governing priority is the preservation of Engineering Judgment, context,
evidence, responsibility, and organizational trust.

> **Foundation Statement:** Every SATCO architecture and implementation decision shall conform to all Non-Negotiable Principles defined by this Manifesto.

## 16. What SATCO Is Not

SATCO is not a Document Management System. Documents may provide evidence and
context, but storing, routing, or controlling documents does not by itself
create Engineering Intelligence.

SATCO is not a traditional Knowledge Management System. It does not treat
knowledge as a collection assembled after work is complete or rely on engineers
to separate learning from the Engineering Work that produced it.

SATCO is not a Computerized Maintenance Management System. Maintenance records
may contribute Engineering Experience, but SATCO does not replace the systems
that govern maintenance execution, assets, work orders, or operational service.

SATCO is not a Project Management Platform. Project plans, schedules, tasks,
and delivery controls may establish relevant context, but they are distinct
from the preservation and development of engineering understanding.

SATCO is not an AI chatbot or a generic AI assistant. Conversation may support
an engineer, but a response detached from governed context, evidence,
responsibility, and approval is not Engineering Intelligence.

SATCO is not a document generator. Producing fluent text or formatted outputs
does not establish that the underlying engineering basis is complete, valid,
or approved.

SATCO is not an AI wrapper. Its identity and enduring value do not arise from
access to a provider or model. Providers assist bounded activities while the
organization retains its knowledge, context, and engineering authority.

SATCO may complement each of these systems without absorbing its purpose. Its
distinct role is to connect Engineering Experience, context, evidence,
relationships, decisions, and Human Approval into a durable organizational
capability.

SATCO is an Engineering Intelligence Platform.

> **Foundation Statement:** SATCO shall remain an Engineering Intelligence Platform and shall not be reduced to document storage, work management, content generation, conversation, or provider access.

## 17. Future Evolution

Engineering Intelligence must evolve because Engineering Work evolves. New
disciplines, operating conditions, obligations, forms of evidence, and modes of
collaboration will create new contexts that the organization must be able to
understand without discarding what it has already learned.

Evolution shall increase the organization's ability to preserve context,
recognize relationships, evaluate evidence, explain implications, and support
Engineering Judgment. Change is valuable when it deepens responsible
understanding rather than merely increasing activity or output.

The platform is designed to adapt while preserving its philosophical
foundation. Its capabilities, boundaries, and methods may change through
governance, but Human Authority, sacred Engineering Context, evidence,
explainability, Provider Independence, and organizational ownership remain
controlling.

Continuous Evolution includes correction and restraint. New understanding may
qualify or supersede prior knowledge, and new capability may reveal that an
earlier approach is no longer sufficient. Governance must preserve the reasons
for change so that progress does not erase engineering history.

The future of SATCO is therefore not predetermined by a specific form of
technology. It is determined by whether the platform continues to strengthen
Collective Engineering Intelligence while remaining accountable to the
permanent principles of this Manifesto.

> **Foundation Statement:** SATCO shall evolve through governance to strengthen Engineering Intelligence while preserving the permanent philosophy, authority, and trust boundaries of the platform.

## 18. Epilogue

Engineering knowledge is created through attention, responsibility, review,
and the accumulated consequences of real Engineering Work. It should never be
lost merely because a Project ends, a team changes, or the systems used to
support the work are replaced.

Organizations become stronger when Engineering Experience remains available as
governed understanding. Preserved context allows prior decisions to be
examined, lessons to be applied responsibly, and new work to begin with a
clearer account of what came before.

Future engineers should begin where previous engineers finished. They should
inherit more than outputs: they should inherit the evidence, rationale,
relationships, uncertainty, and approved knowledge needed to exercise their own
Engineering Judgment.

Engineering Intelligence is the enduring legacy of Engineering Work. It is the
organizational capability that allows experience to remain useful beyond its
original moment while preserving the Human authority that gave it meaning.

Engineering organizations build industrial assets.

SATCO helps them build Engineering Intelligence.

Industrial assets eventually age.

Engineering Intelligence should not.
