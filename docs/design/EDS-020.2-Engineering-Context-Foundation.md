# EDS-020.2: Engineering Context Foundation

## Purpose

This Engineering Design Specification translates accepted ADR-015 into an
implementation-ready, technology-independent contract for PATCH-020.2.

governed engineering meaning inside Projects and Engineering Workspaces.

This document does not implement software and does not define database tables,
SQL, migrations, REST endpoints, transport schemas, repositories, services, or
frontend behavior.

## Canonical Definition

> Engineering Context is the governed, traceable, multidimensional body of
> information needed to understand and perform engineering work inside a
> Project and Engineering Workspace.

Context connects facts, evidence, relationships, conditions, assumptions,
missing information, conflicts, maturity, history, and reviewable
interpretation while preserving human engineering authority.

## Functional Objectives

PATCH-020.2 shall enable authorized engineers to:

- establish shared Project Context without duplicating Project truth;
- establish discipline-bounded Workspace Context;
- connect Context to governed sources and relevant engineering subjects;
- record qualified facts and values with sufficient engineering meaning;
- distinguish authoritative facts, verified facts, assumptions, derived
  findings, AI suggestions, historical references, conflicts, and missing
  information;
- identify who owns information, who stewards its technical meaning, and who
  reviewed it;
- preserve maturity, review, freshness, criticality, confidentiality, source,
  scope, and temporal meaning as independent dimensions;
- expose unresolved conflicts without silently selecting a winner;
- represent Missing Information as an engineering condition rather than an
  empty value;
- express accountable cross-discipline Interface Commitments;
- mark Context stale when decisive evidence or dependencies change;
- preserve corrections, supersession, review, and material history;
- capture selective evidence snapshots for approved material events;
- search and retrieve Context without leaking inaccessible sources;
- provide an authorization-filtered read foundation for later AI capabilities;
- fail safely when information is incomplete, conflicted, stale, unavailable,
  inaccessible, or unsupported.

## Engineering Outcomes

The foundation is successful when an authorized engineer can answer:

- What information governs this work?
- What does the information mean in engineering terms?
- Where did it come from?
- For which Project, Workspace, Discipline, object, stage, and purpose does it
  apply?
- Is it current, mature, reviewed, and sufficiently fresh?
- Who owns and stewards it?
- Which required information is missing?
- Which sources conflict?
- Which other Workspace depends on it?
- What changed, why, and what remains historically true?
- What may AI read, and which limitations must accompany that reading?

## Domain Model

Engineering Context is an aggregate view across native domain subjects and
governed Context elements. It does not own Project, Workspace, Customer,
Document, Decision, Plan, or other shared engineering objects.

The minimum conceptual components are:

### Context Subject

The Project, Workspace, Discipline, engineering object, evidence carrier,
question, review, or decision to which Context applies.

A subject retains its native identity. Context does not create a competing
identity for Project or Workspace.

### Context Element

A bounded unit of contextual meaning associated with one or more subjects.

Context Element is a design term, not a universal Engineering Context Object.
The ECO discovery concluded that native objects, assertions, sources,
conditions, and relationships must retain distinct semantics.

### Context Relationship

A governed statement connecting Context subjects or elements through relevance,
dependency, applicability, evidence, derivation, conflict, supersession,
interface, or impact.

### Context Source Reference

The provenance and bounded standing of evidence supporting a Context element.
It does not replace the source.

### Context Review

A subject-specific human evaluation of a defined Context element, version,
scope, evidence basis, and review question.

### Context Resolution Decision

The minimum Engineering Decision Log foundation for a material Context
resolution. It records a human-owned conclusion, rationale, alternatives where
material, evidence, affected scope, effective meaning, and supersession.

It is not the full future Engineering Decision Log.

### Context Snapshot

A selective immutable evidence frame captured for an approved material event.

### Context History

The preserved evolution of material Context meaning through change,
correction, review, dispute, withdrawal, supersession, and snapshot evidence.

## Context Object Taxonomy

The design uses a taxonomy of distinct Context element kinds. It does not
create one universal ECO type.

### Subject Reference

Establishes relevance to a native Project, Workspace, Discipline, engineering
object, source, or evidence carrier.

### Qualified Fact

A source-supported claim about a subject for a bounded purpose and scope.

### Qualified Engineering Value

A fact whose meaning may require value, unit, quantity type, tolerance, range,
basis, reference condition, operating or design condition, source, revision,
time, and uncertainty.

### Requirement

A Customer, contractual, Project, regulatory, standard-based, or engineering
requirement with source, applicability, precedence, scope, maturity, and review
meaning.

### Applicability Statement

A human-governed statement that a source, requirement, standard, revision,
decision, or historical reference applies or does not apply to a bounded scope.

### Assumption

An explicit provisional human-owned basis with scope, reason, consequence,
criticality, review need, and conditions for confirmation, rejection, or
supersession.

### Missing Information

A first-class condition identifying required meaning that is absent,
incomplete, unknown, inaccessible, or unreviewed.

### Conflict

A first-class condition preserving materially inconsistent claims or
applicability positions.

### Interface Commitment

A governed cross-discipline or external dependency for required information.

### Derived Finding

An explainable conclusion produced from identified inputs.

### Future AI-generated Finding

An explicitly advisory machine-assisted interpretation that may be created
only by PATCH-020.5 or another separately approved AI PATCH. PATCH-020.2
defines its future classification and safety boundary without generating or
persisting it.

### Historical Reference

Prior evidence or understanding preserved with original source, scope,
authority, maturity, review, time, and reuse limits.

### Source and Evidence Reference

A contextual link to the carrier or origin of evidence. It remains distinct
from the claim supported by that evidence.

### Context Resolution Decision Reference

A link to the human decision that resolves or qualifies current Context.

The taxonomy is allow-listed. A sub-patch may implement only the kinds
explicitly assigned to it.

## Context Dimensions

Every Context element shall support the dimensions relevant to its kind.
Dimensions are independent and not one exclusive state.

- **Authority:** why the element may be relied upon for a bounded purpose.
- **Derivation:** direct, calculated, derived, or AI-assisted origin.
- **Temporality:** current, future-effective, expired, superseded, withdrawn,
  or historical meaning.
- **Review state:** human evaluation standing for a defined review question.
- **Maturity:** readiness for intended use.
- **Freshness:** whether the element remains current enough for its purpose.
- **Criticality:** consequence of incorrect, missing, stale, or conflicting
  meaning.
- **Confidentiality:** access and reuse restrictions.
- **Source:** origin, owner, evidence, and revision.
- **Scope:** applicable Customer, Project, Workspace, Discipline, subject,
  location, lifecycle stage, purpose, and condition.

One dimension must not silently determine another.

## Relationships Among Context Elements

The foundation shall support these conceptual relationship meanings:

- **applies to:** Context element is relevant to a subject or scope;
- **evidenced by:** claim or finding cites a source;
- **derived from:** derived element cites all decisive inputs;
- **requires:** work or element depends on required information;
- **provided by:** Interface Commitment identifies provider;
- **consumed by:** Interface Commitment identifies consumer;
- **conflicts with:** claims remain competing and visible;
- **supersedes:** later governed meaning replaces earlier current use;
- **corrects:** later meaning repairs an error while preserving the original;
- **withdraws:** information is removed from current reliance;
- **reviews:** Human Review evaluates a specific version and question;
- **decides:** human resolution establishes current bounded meaning;
- **snapshot of:** evidence frame preserves the basis of a material event;
- **potentially affects:** derived impact relation requires review;
- **historical precedent for:** prior Context is suggested with reuse limits.

Relationships are directional where provider, consumer, source, derivation,
supersession, correction, or decision meaning requires direction.

Relationships never silently transfer ownership, authority, maturity, or
approval.

## Ownership Model

### Information owner

Responsible for maintenance, availability, and controlled change.

### Source owner

Responsible for the native source and its issue or revision process.

### Engineering steward

An active internal User explicitly designated as responsible for technical
meaning, applicability, completeness, and quality within a defined scope.

### Reviewer

An active internal User explicitly designated to evaluate a Context subject
within stated competence and authorization.

### Approver

Only a separately governed authority may formally approve a subject. PATCH-020.2
does not create formal deliverable approval.

### Project responsibility

Project owner governs shared Project Context participation and designates
stewardship for Project-scoped elements.

### Workspace responsibility

Workspace owner governs discipline-local Context participation and designates
stewardship within that Workspace.

Primary assignees and collaborators may contribute within authorization but do
not gain verification authority merely through participation.

### Competence rule

Role, ownership, assignment, collaboration, and administration do not prove
technical competence.

The first release records explicit human responsibility. It does not attempt to
certify professional qualifications. The accountable human and organization
remain responsible for appropriate designation.

## Authority Model

The initial authority standings are:

- **Authoritative Fact:** supported by a governed source or process for bounded
  use;
- **Engineer-Verified Fact:** checked by a designated steward or reviewer
  against suitable evidence;
- **Assumption:** provisional human-owned basis;
- **Derived Finding:** sourced interpretation without independent authority;
- **AI Suggestion:** advisory machine-assisted interpretation;
- **Historical Reference:** authentic prior evidence without automatic current
  applicability;
- **Unresolved Conflict:** competing claims with no current silent winner;
- **Missing Information:** required meaning not currently available for use.

Authority is explicit and traceable.

Only:

- a governed source event;
- a completed authorized Human Review;
- or a human Context Resolution Decision

may establish or change authority standing.

AI Confidence, recency, frequency, ownership, and administrator role cannot
promote authority.

## Review Model

Human Review is subject-specific. Each review evaluates:

- one defined Context subject or element version;
- one bounded scope;
- one review question;
- cited evidence;
- known assumptions, conflicts, and missing information;
- reviewer identity and responsibility;
- outcome;
- rationale where material;
- time.

Initial review outcomes are:

- **Unreviewed**
- **Needs More Information**
- **Verified by Engineer**
- **Rejected**
- **Disputed**
- **Superseded**

These outcomes align with ADR-014 but do not create a universal review workflow.

### Review rules

- AI cannot review its own output.
- Review does not equal formal deliverable approval.
- Review of one element does not approve connected elements.
- Review applies to the exact version and scope evaluated.
- Changed decisive evidence makes the review stale or superseded for affected
  use.
- Completed review evidence is preserved.
- Reconsideration produces a later review rather than rewriting the completed
  outcome.

### Required review thresholds

Independent Human Review is required before:

- a future AI-generated finding affects engineering action after a separately
  approved AI PATCH introduces such findings;
- a Derived Finding is promoted to Engineer-Verified standing;
- a conflict is resolved;
- a material assumption is accepted for continued use;
- High Impact or Safety Critical Missing Information is marked resolved;
- High Impact or Safety Critical Interface Commitment is marked fulfilled for
  consumer reliance;
- a material correction establishes current use;
- a snapshot supports a decision or formal review;
- an element becomes Approved for Use maturity.

“Independent” means a designated reviewer other than AI. This EDS does not
mandate a second human different from the contributor for every case; any
separation-of-duty requirement belongs to the applicable engineering or
approval governance.

## Maturity Model

Initial conceptual maturity states are:

- **Preliminary:** early information not yet suitable for unqualified reliance;
- **In Development:** actively being developed or completed;
- **Verified:** checked for a bounded engineering purpose;
- **Approved for Use:** accepted through an applicable human-governed process
  for stated use, without implying formal deliverable approval;
- **Superseded:** replaced for current use;
- **Historical:** retained as prior evidence.

### Maturity rules

- Maturity is independent from authority, review, and freshness.
- AI cannot change maturity.
- Material maturity changes identify actor, basis, time, and scope.
- Maturity may differ by Workspace and purpose.
- A source may be authentic while preliminary.
- Approved-for-use information may become stale or superseded.
- Missing Information and Conflict use condition-specific states rather than
  being forced through every maturity state.

## Freshness Model

Initial freshness states are:

- **Current:** sufficiently current for stated use;
- **Review Due:** still visible for use subject to required reassessment;
- **Stale:** current-use basis is no longer sufficient;
- **Superseded:** a later governed revision or meaning governs current use;
- **Unknown:** freshness cannot be established.

### Freshness basis

Each element identifies the applicable freshness basis:

- source revision;
- effective or observation time;
- review-by condition where justified;
- Project stage;
- dependency state;
- criticality;
- operating or design condition;
- governing decision;
- intended use.

### Initial invalidation triggers

An element is reassessed when:

- its source revision changes;
- a decisive input changes;
- its subject or applicability scope changes;
- Project or Workspace stage changes materially;
- an Interface Commitment changes or is withdrawn;
- a governing requirement or decision changes;
- a correction or conflict affects its basis;
- its review-by condition is reached;
- access to required evidence is lost;
- a designated steward explicitly marks the basis uncertain.

There is no universal time-to-live.

Stale, superseded, and unknown Context remains visible with clear standing and
cannot support unqualified AI or human conclusions.

## Criticality Model

Initial conceptual criticality levels are:

- **Informational:** little engineering consequence if delayed or imperfect;
- **Important:** meaningful effect on understanding or coordination;
- **High Impact:** material effect on engineering direction, rework, cost,
  review, or connected work;
- **Safety Critical:** potential effect on human safety, plant safety, or a
  safety-significant engineering basis.

### Criticality rules

- The contributor proposes criticality with rationale.
- The designated steward confirms High Impact criticality.
- A designated qualified reviewer confirms Safety Critical criticality.
- Criticality may vary by scope and consumer.
- Criticality affects visibility, review priority, freshness expectation,
  snapshot justification, audit significance, and AI caution.
- Criticality does not create truth, authority, or approval.
- No universal numeric score is introduced.

## Source Model

Initial source kinds are:

- Customer document or instruction;
- contract;
- approved Project document;
- vendor document or submission;
- site survey or field observation;
- standard or code reference;
- calculation;
- engineer input;
- external reference;
- historical Project evidence;
- AI-derived interpretation.

Every material source reference identifies, where applicable:

- source kind;
- native identity or stable reference;
- source owner;
- revision or issue;
- effective or observation time;
- maturity or issue standing;
- confidentiality;
- accessible evidence;
- applicability scope;
- known limitations.

### Precedence rules

- No universal ranking exists.
- Precedence is explicit for the exact question and scope.
- Applicable contract, Customer direction, approved Project basis, qualified
  engineering evidence, standards, revision, and effective time are considered.
- A precedence conclusion requires designated human responsibility.
- Competing sources remain visible.
- A higher-precedence conclusion does not erase lower-precedence evidence.
- Specialized source hierarchies require separate architecture approval and
  are outside this EDS.

## Conflict Model

A Conflict exists when two or more claims, values, relationships, or
applicability positions disagree materially for the same purpose.

Each Conflict identifies:

- competing claims without overwriting them;
- all known source references and revisions;
- affected Project, Workspaces, subjects, and scope;
- owner and steward;
- criticality;
- freshness impact;
- review state;
- current safe-use limitation;
- potential affected elements;
- resolution decision when available;
- historical evolution.

Initial conflict states are:

- **Identified**
- **Under Review**
- **Needs More Information**
- **Resolved**
- **Disputed**
- **Superseded**

### Resolution

Resolution requires Human Review and, when the result materially determines
engineering direction or applicability, a Context Resolution Decision.

Resolution:

- states the current bounded conclusion;
- records rationale and evidence;
- preserves all competing sources;
- identifies affected elements requiring reassessment;
- never relies on hidden precedence;
- never permits AI to choose the winner.

Unresolved material conflict produces **Conflict Requires Review**.

## Missing Information Model

Missing Information is not null. It is a governed engineering condition.

Initial missing kinds are:

- required input absent;
- source unavailable;
- value incomplete;
- unit or reference condition missing;
- revision unknown;
- owner or steward unknown;
- review incomplete;
- applicability unknown;
- Interface Commitment unmet;
- access restricted.

Each Missing Information condition identifies:

- required meaning;
- requirement basis;
- affected subject and scope;
- owning or expected provider where known;
- consumer;
- criticality;
- consequence;
- stage or due condition where applicable;
- current status;
- evidence of resolution;
- review requirement.

Initial states are:

- **Identified**
- **Acknowledged**
- **Information Requested**
- **Provided — Review Required**
- **Resolved**
- **Not Applicable**
- **Superseded**

Resolution requires evidence that the required meaning is complete enough for
the stated purpose. High Impact and Safety Critical resolution requires Human
Review.

Missing Information may block:

- authority promotion;
- maturity progression;
- conflict resolution;
- Interface Commitment fulfilment;
- later Plan, Health, Readiness, AI Insight, or ENSE use.

## Historical Model

Material Context history preserves:

- prior values and qualified assertions;
- source and revision history;
- prior applicability;
- assumptions and their resolution;
- conflicts and resolution;
- Missing Information and resolution;
- review outcomes;
- maturity and authority changes;
- freshness and staleness events;
- Interface Commitment changes;
- corrections, withdrawals, and supersession;
- future AI-generated findings and human response, when introduced by a
  separately approved AI PATCH;
- snapshots;
- Context Resolution Decisions.

### Historical rules

- Current and historical meaning are visibly distinct.
- Supersession never destroys the prior state.
- Withdrawal removes current reliance, not historical evidence.
- Correction preserves original and corrected meaning.
- Historical evidence retains original confidentiality and scope.
- Historical reuse requires current applicability and authorization review.
- Ordinary Context operations do not physically erase material history.
- Retention and cross-Customer reuse beyond existing governance remain outside
  this EDS and require separate approval.

## Derived Context Model

Initial derived kinds are:

- missing-input detection;
- conflict detection;
- stale-data warning;
- cross-discipline dependency finding;
- potential impact relation;
- completeness observation;
- review-priority suggestion;
- applicability candidate;
- historical-similarity candidate.

Each Derived Finding identifies:

- exact inputs and versions;
- derivation kind;
- rule, method, or reasoning basis in understandable engineering terms;
- assumptions;
- scope;
- assessment time;
- freshness state;
- criticality;
- limitations;
- responsible human steward;
- review outcome where material.

Derived Context is reproducible where the method and available inputs permit.
If inputs change, it is reassessed or marked stale.

Derived Context remains non-authoritative unless an authorized Human Review
explicitly establishes a bounded Engineer-Verified standing. The original
derivation remains visible.

## AI-generated Context Model

This section defines future architectural classification and safety semantics
only.

PATCH-020.2 does not generate, persist, review, or promote AI-generated Context
findings. AI output generation, including AI Insights and AI-generated Context
findings, is deferred to PATCH-020.5 or another separately approved AI PATCH.
PATCH-020.2 prepares only an authorized read layer for future AI use.

A future AI-generated finding shall identify:

- question or detection purpose;
- Project and Workspace scope;
- source inputs and versions;
- assumptions and missing information;
- Engineering Reasoning suitable for review;
- confidence and its basis;
- model/provider/version provenance where material;
- assessment time;
- freshness;
- criticality;
- Human Review requirement;
- supersession.

Future AI may:

- summarize authorized sources;
- identify possible missing information;
- detect potential conflicts;
- suggest relationships;
- identify historical similarity;
- propose questions;
- estimate confidence;
- recommend Human Review.

Future AI may not:

- create or modify authoritative facts;
- promote authority, maturity, or approval;
- resolve conflict;
- fulfil Interface Commitments;
- mark material Missing Information resolved;
- invent facts, standards, sources, vendor data, or history;
- silently merge information;
- hide uncertainty or access limitations;
- expose inaccessible sources;
- create human decisions;
- modify historical evidence.

When a separately approved AI PATCH introduces them, material AI-generated
findings require Human Review before engineering action.

## Snapshot Model

Snapshots are selective immutable evidence frames.

### Initial approved triggers

PATCH-020.2 may capture a snapshot for:

- completed material Context Review;
- material Context Resolution Decision;
- resolution of a High Impact or Safety Critical Conflict;
- material correction or dispute resolution;
- explicit authorized stage-boundary evidence request.

Future triggers for Engineering Execution Plan versions, AI Insights, ENSE, or
formal approvals belong to their future domains.

### Snapshot content boundary

A snapshot contains only:

- subject and scope;
- decisive Context elements and versions;
- decisive sources and revisions;
- assumptions;
- conflicts;
- Missing Information;
- authority, maturity, review, freshness, and criticality standing;
- relationships necessary to understand the decision or review;
- assessment time;
- actor and trigger;
- known access restrictions.

It does not indiscriminately copy the Project, Workspace, every connected
object, or full source content.

### Snapshot rules

- Snapshot purpose is explicit.
- Snapshot content is explainable and bounded.
- Snapshot is immutable.
- Later evidence creates a later Context or snapshot.
- Source confidentiality and authorization remain effective.
- Snapshot evidence is historical, not current truth.
- Retention follows existing governance until separately approved.

## Minimal Human Review and Decision Foundation

PATCH-020.2 requires enough shared foundation to support:

- Context verification;
- conflict resolution;
- material assumption acceptance;
- critical Missing Information resolution;
- material correction;
- Interface Commitment fulfilment review;
- Derived Finding review;
- snapshot evidence.

The minimum Context Resolution Decision records:

- decision question;
- human decision statement;
- accountable actor;
- scope and effective meaning;
- rationale;
- alternatives where material;
- decisive evidence and source revisions;
- assumptions and uncertainty;
- affected subjects, Workspaces, conflicts, or elements;
- review evidence;
- time;
- supersession.

Non-scope includes generalized decision categories, full Decision Log search,
formal approval workflows, broad Decision Log UI, and decision behavior for
future Plan, Health, Insight, or Memory domains.

## Interface Commitment Rules

The first release supports information-delivery commitments only.

An Interface Commitment identifies:

- provider Project participant, Workspace, or external source;
- consuming Workspace;
- required information;
- semantic completeness expectations;
- source expectation;
- stage or due condition;
- criticality;
- steward;
- reviewer;
- confidentiality;
- current state;
- change impact;
- related Missing Information.

Initial states are:

- **Identified**
- **Acknowledged by Provider**
- **Information Provided**
- **Consumer Review Required**
- **Fulfilled for Stated Use**
- **Rejected**
- **Disputed**
- **Superseded**

### Rules

- Provider acknowledgement does not imply information is complete.
- Information Provided does not imply consumer acceptance.
- Fulfilment requires consumer-side Human Review for Important, High Impact,
  and Safety Critical commitments.
- Fulfilment is bounded to stated use, source, and revision.
- A source revision reopens consumer review where material.
- Provider and consumer disagreements remain Conflict.
- Commitment state does not alter source authority automatically.
- No task assignment, notification, workflow, schedule, or automatic escalation
  is introduced.
- No Workspace silently fulfils another Workspace’s responsibility.

## Audit Requirements

Every material Context event shall create centralized audit evidence.

Required event meanings include:

- Context element recorded;
- qualified value changed;
- source linked, changed, or removed from current reliance;
- owner or steward changed;
- authority changed;
- maturity changed;
- review completed;
- freshness changed materially;
- criticality changed;
- Conflict identified, disputed, resolved, or superseded;
- Missing Information identified, resolved, made not applicable, or
  superseded;
- Interface Commitment created, acknowledged, provided, reviewed, fulfilled,
  disputed, rejected, or superseded;
- correction recorded;
- information withdrawn or superseded;
- Derived Finding created or invalidated;
- Context Resolution Decision recorded or superseded;
- snapshot captured;
- confidentiality changed.

Audit evidence identifies:

- actor;
- event;
- subject;
- Project and Workspace scope;
- before and after meaning where material;
- reason;
- source or evidence references without unnecessary sensitive content;
- resulting version or historical state;
- time.

Failed, rejected, unauthorized, validation-failed, and concurrency-conflicted
mutations create no success audit evidence.

Audit records do not replace domain history, review evidence, decisions, or
snapshots.

## Search Requirements

Authorized Context discovery shall support:

- Project and Workspace scope;
- Discipline;
- subject identity or reference;
- Context kind;
- source kind and source reference;
- owner and steward;
- authority standing;
- maturity;
- review state;
- freshness;
- criticality;
- Conflict state;
- Missing Information state;
- Interface Commitment provider, consumer, and state;
- current versus historical standing;
- terms present in qualified textual meaning where authorized.

### Search rules

- Authentication is required.
- Authorization is applied before totals and pagination.
- Derived results do not reveal inaccessible sources.
- Restricted Context does not leak through counts, facets, snippets, or
  relationships.
- Normal search favors current Context.
- Historical discovery is explicit and clearly labeled.
- Stale and conflicted current Context remains discoverable with visible state.
- Archived Workspace Context is excluded from normal current search and is
  available only through explicit authorized history discovery.
- Deterministic ordering is required.
- Existing Customer, Contact, Project, Project Code, and Workspace search
  behavior remains compatible.

This EDS defines search behavior, not transport interfaces.

## Authorization Requirements

Only current roles `admin` and `engineer` exist. PATCH-020.2 adds no roles.

Authorization combines current role with:

- Project ownership or primary assignment;
- Workspace ownership or primary assignment;
- Workspace collaboration;
- explicit information ownership;
- explicit engineering stewardship;
- explicit reviewer designation;
- source confidentiality;
- Project and Workspace scope.

### Capability boundary

| Capability | Admin | Project owner | Workspace owner | Primary assignee | Collaborator | Designated steward/reviewer |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| View authorized Project Context | Any, subject to source restriction | Yes | Relevant scope | Relevant scope | Relevant Workspace scope | Assigned scope |
| Record preliminary Context | Yes | Project scope | Workspace scope | Assigned scope | Workspace contribution | Assigned scope |
| Change shared Project applicability | Administrative only unless designated | Govern and designate | No automatic right | No automatic right | No | If designated |
| Change Workspace applicability | Administrative only unless designated | Govern and designate | Govern and designate | Contribute | Contribute | If designated |

| Manage Interface Commitment | Administer | Project coordination | Workspace provider/consumer scope | Assigned participation | Contribute | Review assigned scope |

| View restricted source meaning | Only when separately authorized | Only when source permits | Only when source permits | Only when source permits | Only when source permits | Only when source permits |

Admin authority permits administration and recovery but does not imply
engineering competence.

### Non-disclosure

- Permission is checked before mutation and disclosure.
- Unauthorized Context identifiers do not reveal existence where that would
  leak information.
- Cross-Workspace access uses least privilege.
- Consumer access to a commitment does not grant access to unrelated provider
  Context.
- AI reads no more than the requesting actor may read.
- Search totals and derived views apply the same restrictions.

## Cross-Workspace Rules

- Shared Project facts remain Project Context.
- Native engineering subjects retain one identity.
- Workspace-specific interpretations remain separately attributable.
- A Workspace references shared Context rather than copying it.
- Provider and consumer responsibilities are explicit.
- Cross-Workspace relationships preserve source, revision, confidentiality,
  stewardship, review, and conflict.
- A consumer cannot alter provider-owned source meaning.
- A provider cannot declare consumer review complete.
- Changes trigger visible reassessment for affected consumers.
- Cross-Workspace conflict preserves each accountable interpretation.
- Authorization is evaluated for every connected source; relationship access
  does not grant unrestricted traversal.
- Archiving a Workspace preserves its historical commitments, reviews,
  decisions, and Context without leaving them in normal operational results.

## Performance Expectations

Performance must support engineering orientation without weakening governance.

### Bounded retrieval

- Current Context retrieval is bounded by Project, Workspace, subject, or
  explicit engineering question.
- Collections are paginated.
- Evidence detail and history may be progressively retrieved.
- Context assembly does not traverse every connected historical object by
  default.
- Normal current views avoid loading full snapshots or full source content.

### Predictability

- Deterministic ordering is required for repeated retrieval.
- Authorization filtering occurs within the result computation, not after
  unbounded retrieval.
- Totals describe only authorized results.
- Derived and stale-state evaluation avoids repeated recomputation when its
  governed inputs have not changed.
- A single highly connected subject must not cause unbounded traversal.

### Validation expectation

Each implementation sub-patch shall define measured response and scale targets
against an approved representative isolated dataset before implementation
approval. No universal latency target is invented by this technology-independent
EDS.

Safety, authorization, source meaning, and traceability cannot be weakened to
improve response time.

## Traceability Requirements

An authorized reviewer must be able to reconstruct:

- what the Context element meant;
- which subject and scope it applied to;
- source identity, revision, and standing;
- value, unit, basis, conditions, and uncertainty where relevant;
- owner, steward, reviewer, and decision actor;
- authority, maturity, review, freshness, and criticality at the material time;
- relationships and decisive dependencies;
- missing information and conflicts;
- derivation inputs and reasoning;
- AI provenance and confidence where applicable;
- correction, withdrawal, or supersession;
- snapshot basis;
- human decision and rationale;
- historical evolution.

Traceability survives the immediate interaction and does not depend on chat
history, navigation history, or generic audit details alone.

## Safe Failure Requirements

The foundation uses explicit safe outcomes:

- **Insufficient Information**
- **Conflict Requires Review**
- **Source Not Available**
- **Context Is Stale**
- **Access Restricted**

Safe failure:

- identifies the limiting condition;
- states affected scope and consequence;
- preserves known evidence;
- avoids unsupported defaults;
- prevents authority or maturity promotion;
- prevents AI from presenting an unqualified conclusion;
- recommends the responsible information or review need where known;
- does not leak inaccessible sources.

## Concurrency and Change Integrity

Every mutation of material Context meaning shall:

- identify the expected current version or equivalent concurrency basis;
- detect conflicting concurrent changes;
- preserve successful ordering;
- reject stale mutation without partial success;
- avoid success audit evidence when rejected;
- preserve source and relationship consistency;
- ensure review, decision, correction, and snapshot evidence refers to the
  exact subject version evaluated.

This is a behavioral contract, not a persistence design.

## Compatibility With Workspace Core

PATCH-020.2 preserves PATCH-020.1:

- one Workspace identity per Project and Discipline;
- derived Workspace display name;
- Workspace owner, optional primary assignee, and collaborators;
- Workspace lifecycle and archival state;
- optimistic concurrency;
- permission-scoped visibility;
- centralized audit integration;
- authorization-filtered Universal Search;
- Project deletion guard after Workspace history;
- no physical Workspace deletion;
- only `admin` and `engineer` roles.

### Operational compatibility

- Context is never created automatically merely because a Workspace exists.
- Workspace creation remains valid without Context.
- Context cannot change Workspace Discipline.
- Context does not replace Workspace description.
- Workspace archive preserves Context history.
- Archived Context does not appear in normal operational search.
- Project and Workspace ownership does not automatically grant technical
  verification competence.
- Context source and subject references do not transfer native ownership.
- No Context mutation silently changes Project or Workspace lifecycle.

## Compatibility With Future Domains

### Engineering Decision Log

PATCH-020.2 establishes only the minimum Context Resolution Decision semantics.
The future full Log may extend categories, relationships, discovery, review,
and supersession without rewriting existing decision evidence.

### Engineering Execution Plan

The Plan consumes current authorized Context, Missing Information, Conflicts,
Interface Commitments, maturity, freshness, and snapshots. Plan versions
preserve their input basis. The Plan never becomes source authority.

### AI Insights

Insights cite exact authorized Context elements, sources, snapshots, assumptions,
conflicts, and missing information. Insight review does not mutate Context
silently.

### ENSE

ENSE uses Context to propose next actions with dependencies and confidence. It
does not execute commitments, resolve missing information, or change maturity.

### Engineering Memory

Only reviewed decisions, outcomes, revisions, and lessons with known scope may
become Memory. Raw Context and unreviewed AI findings remain ineligible.

### Engineering Health and Workspace Readiness

Health and Readiness derive explainable indicators and blockers from versioned
Context. They do not alter their inputs or imply formal approval.

### Engineering Knowledge Graph

Context subjects and relationships remain technology-independent. A future
Knowledge Graph may connect them without changing authority, scope,
confidentiality, history, or native identity.

### Documents

Documents remain controlled sources and evidence carriers. Context references
documents and revisions without becoming a document repository or duplicating
document governance.

## Electrical Validation Scenario

A new 160 kW process pump requires Electrical engineering work. No engineering
calculation or selection is performed.

The EDS supports:

- shared Project Context for Customer, plant, area, Project stage, and common
  requirements;
- Electrical Workspace Context for motor identity, rated power, voltage basis,
  duty, starting information, short-circuit input, Customer standard, and
  installation conditions;
- qualified motor power with value, unit, quantity meaning, source, revision,
  condition, and review;
- a Mechanical-to-Electrical Interface Commitment for confirmed motor data;
- Missing Information for absent vendor datasheet;
- Conflict where Customer standard and Project basis disagree;
- stale Electrical derivations after a new Mechanical revision;
- High Impact criticality where incorrect source data materially affects
  connected work;
- explainable Derived Findings for missing-input and potential-impact review;
- Human Review of governing electrical sources;
- correction that preserves erroneous and corrected value;
- supersession that preserves legitimate prior design revisions;
- selective snapshot of a material resolution decision.

## Instrumentation Validation Scenario

A flowmeter requires pressure and temperature compensation. No calculation,

The EDS supports:

- Missing Information for absent reference condition;

- explainable Derived Findings that preserve the missing reference condition

- qualified Process and Instrumentation Human Review;
- historical preservation of prior sources, warning, conflict, and resolution;
- snapshot of decisive evidence for a material Context resolution.
