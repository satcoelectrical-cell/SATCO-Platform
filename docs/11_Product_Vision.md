# SATCO Product Vision

## Purpose

This document defines the enduring product vision for SATCO Platform. It establishes the product’s identity, mission, intended users, direction of evolution, and standard for success. It is a mandatory reference for future product decisions and PATCH proposals.

## Scope

This vision governs SATCO as an AI-powered Engineering Copilot Platform. It describes what the product should enable and how it should evolve without prescribing implementation, data structures, interfaces, or delivery schedules.

## Definitions

- **Engineering Copilot:** An intelligence layer that helps engineers analyze context, identify risks, evaluate options, and make better decisions without replacing professional judgment.
- **Engineering Workspace:** A discipline-oriented environment containing engineering context, work, evidence, decisions, risks, and reviews.
- **Engineering Execution Plan:** A continuously evolving engineering roadmap that recommends phases, required inputs, risks, deliverables, effort, and next steps. It is not a project schedule.
- **Engineering Intelligence:** Contextual analysis that connects engineering information, standards, history, and relationships to produce explainable recommendations.
- **Engineering Memory:** Governed organizational knowledge formed from reviewed decisions, outcomes, revisions, and lessons learned.

## Long-Term Vision

SATCO will become the trusted working environment in which engineering organizations understand their projects, preserve their knowledge, recognize risks earlier, and make decisions with stronger context.

Its long-term value is not the automatic production of engineering outputs. Its value is the disciplined connection of engineering information, reasoning, history, and human review across the lifecycle of a project.

SATCO should make complex engineering work easier to understand without pretending that complexity does not exist. It should help engineers see what is known, what is missing, what conflicts, what may be affected, and what deserves attention next.

## Product Mission

SATCO assists engineers throughout the engineering lifecycle by:

- organizing engineering work around meaningful discipline and project context;
- analyzing connected engineering information;
- explaining implications, uncertainty, and evidence;
- recommending next actions and review priorities;
- preserving reviewed decisions and lessons;
- improving organizational readiness without weakening human responsibility.

Engineering judgment always remains with the engineer.

## Target Users

SATCO is intended for people accountable for engineering quality and execution, including:

- discipline engineers and lead engineers;
- project engineers and engineering managers;
- reviewers, approvers, and technical authorities;
- commissioning, operations, and maintenance engineers;
- document and information coordinators serving engineering work;
- organizational leaders responsible for engineering risk, capacity, and learning.

Each role may need a different view, but all views must refer to the same governed engineering context.

## Global Positioning

SATCO occupies the space between fragmented engineering information and human engineering judgment. It is neither a design-authoring system nor a conversational assistant detached from project reality.

SATCO is positioned as:

- an engineering context and intelligence platform;
- a workspace for analysis, review, readiness, and decision support;
- a governed memory of engineering rationale and outcomes;
- a bridge across disciplines, project stages, standards, and organizational history.

It must complement established engineering tools and processes while remaining clear about its own responsibility boundaries.

## Engineering Workspace Vision

Projects are expressed through Engineering Workspaces such as Electrical, Instrumentation, Control, Mechanical, and Civil. A workspace is not a chat history. It is the engineer’s coherent view of scope, inputs, equipment, tags, documents, calculations, risks, reviews, decisions, and readiness.

Workspaces must support both discipline depth and cross-discipline awareness. They should reduce navigation effort, expose relevant context at the point of work, and make dependencies visible before they become late-stage problems.

## Engineering Execution Plan Vision

After project creation, SATCO should suggest an Engineering Execution Plan based on available project context, discipline, scope, standards, customer expectations, and reviewed historical experience.

The plan should:

- identify suggested engineering phases and deliverables;
- expose required and missing inputs;
- highlight potential risks and critical dependencies;
- recommend the next engineering step;
- estimate effort and appropriate team composition with visible confidence;
- identify relevant historical projects;
- evolve as context, reviews, and decisions change.

The plan guides engineering attention. It does not approve work, assign professional responsibility, or replace formal scheduling.

## Engineering Intelligence Vision

Engineering Intelligence should operate over relationships rather than isolated text. Its recommendations should reflect the relevant customer, plant, area, discipline, system, equipment, tag, document, revision, standard, decision history, and project stage.

Every meaningful recommendation should answer:

1. What was observed?
2. Why does it matter?
3. What evidence and context were considered?
4. What is uncertain or missing?
5. What action is recommended?
6. Who must review or decide?

## Future Evolution

SATCO should evolve from governed contextual assistance toward deeper multidisciplinary intelligence. Progress should occur through increased context quality, traceability, review discipline, and organizational learning—not through the removal of human accountability.

Future capabilities may strengthen:

- lifecycle-wide engineering readiness;
- cross-discipline conflict and impact analysis;
- standards awareness;
- similarity-based organizational learning;
- portfolio-level engineering risk visibility;
- multilingual and regional engineering context;
- collaboration across customers, vendors, and engineering organizations.

## Success Definition

SATCO succeeds when it measurably helps engineering organizations:

- make better-supported decisions;
- detect missing information and conflicts earlier;
- reduce avoidable engineering risk and rework;
- understand readiness with greater confidence;
- preserve rationale across people and project phases;
- reuse reviewed experience responsibly;
- maintain clear human ownership of every engineering decision.

Usage volume, recommendation count, or automation rate alone are not measures of product success.

## Ten-Year Vision

Within ten years, SATCO should serve as a durable engineering intelligence environment across project types, disciplines, regions, and lifecycle stages. An engineer entering a project should be able to understand its context, dependencies, decisions, unresolved risks, and readiness without reconstructing its history from disconnected sources.

Organizations should be able to learn from completed work while respecting differences in customer requirements, standards, operating environments, and engineering judgment. SATCO should become a trusted institutional memory that strengthens engineers rather than making them dependent on opaque automation.

## Design Rules

1. Every product capability must improve an engineering decision, reduce engineering risk, or strengthen engineering understanding.
2. Context must precede recommendation.
3. Recommendations must remain advisory, explainable, and reviewable.
4. Uncertainty and missing information must remain visible.
5. Engineering Workspaces, not conversations, are the primary product environment.
6. Reviewed decisions and outcomes form memory; unreviewed AI output does not.
7. Product evolution must preserve backward understanding of prior engineering rationale.
8. Simplicity must clarify engineering work, not conceal important complexity.

## Examples

- When required project inputs are missing, SATCO identifies the gap, explains its likely impact, and recommends who should review it.
- When two disciplines depend on the same equipment attribute, SATCO exposes the relationship and a potential conflict without choosing the engineering resolution.
- When a historical project appears similar, SATCO explains the basis of similarity and the important differences before suggesting its relevance.

## Future Implications

All future PATCHes must state how they support this vision, which engineering problem they address, what context they require, how human review is preserved, and how their outcomes contribute to traceable Engineering Memory.
